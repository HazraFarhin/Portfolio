## Tools Used

- Figma
- FigJam
- Zeroheight
- Maze

## Outcome & Impact

- Stakeholders approved the redesigned workflow for the next release cycle
- Reduced the number of steps required to complete the primary task flow
- Established a shared component library the engineering team could implement directly

## The Challenge

- New users struggled to learn the tool's core workflow, leading to a steep onboarding curve
- Advanced users relied on dense, expert-level controls that any redesign risked removing
- Inconsistent UI patterns across the product made the tool feel harder to trust than it was

## Process

### 1. Discovery & Research

Reviewed existing product analytics and held stakeholder workshops to understand where users were getting stuck in the core workflow.

### 2. Define

Synthesized research into problem statements and a small set of user flows that framed the redesign priorities.

### 3. Ideate & Wireframe

Explored several low-fidelity directions for the primary workflow before narrowing in on an approach that balanced simplicity with expert-level control.

### 4. Design & Prototype

Built high-fidelity screens and a small interaction system, translating the chosen direction into a testable prototype.

### 5. Test & Iterate

Ran usability sessions with a mix of new and experienced users, then iterated on the flow based on where testers hesitated or backtracked.

## Solution

The redesigned workflow keeps the tool's expert-level controls intact while giving new users a clearer path through the primary task. Shared components carried the new visual language consistently across the product.

## Learnings & Reflections

Balancing power-user needs against onboarding friction took longer than expected -- an early version simplified too aggressively and had to be walked back after testing. In hindsight, involving experienced users in the earliest wireframe reviews, rather than only at the testing stage, would have caught that tension sooner.
