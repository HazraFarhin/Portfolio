## Tools Used

- Figma
- FigJam
- Maze
- Notion

## Outcome & Impact

- Stakeholders approved the redesigned flow for the next release cycle
- Reduced the number of taps required to complete the most common transaction
- Aligned interaction patterns across the app's core banking flows

## The Challenge

- Everyday banking tasks required more steps than users expected, creating friction in frequent flows
- Interaction patterns varied across different parts of the app, undermining consistency
- Stakeholders had differing opinions on prioritization, requiring careful facilitation to reach alignment

> The interface needed to feel like one coherent product, not five different tools stitched together under one login.

## Process

### 1. Discovery & Research

Reviewed usage patterns and held stakeholder interviews to identify which banking flows caused the most friction.

### 2. Define

Framed the redesign around the highest-frequency transactions, prioritizing flows used most often by everyday customers.

### 3. Ideate & Wireframe

Explored simplified flow structures for the core transactions, testing different levels of step consolidation.

### 4. Design & Prototype

Designed high-fidelity screens for the revised flows and built an interactive prototype for stakeholder and user review.

### 5. Test & Iterate

Gathered feedback across multiple stakeholder review cycles and refined the flow based on where reviewers flagged confusion or risk.

## Solution

The redesigned flows consolidate the most common banking tasks into fewer, clearer steps, with consistent interaction patterns carried across the app. Working solo meant stakeholder reviews doubled as a lightweight design-critique loop throughout the project.

## Learnings & Reflections

Working without a dedicated PM meant stakeholder alignment took more of my own time than expected, but it also meant I stayed closer to the reasoning behind each decision. In hindsight, I'd set up a lighter-weight review cadence earlier instead of batching feedback into a few large sessions.
