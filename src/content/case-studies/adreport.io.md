## Outcome & Impact

- Stakeholders approved the redesigned dashboard for the next release cycle
- Reduced the number of custom reports users needed to build to see their key metrics
- Simplified the dashboard's information hierarchy around the most-checked campaign metrics

## The Challenge

- Campaign managers had to build custom reports just to see the metrics they checked daily
- The dashboard's information hierarchy buried frequently used data beneath rarely used controls
- Working solo meant balancing design decisions against engineering and data constraints without a dedicated PM

> The metrics campaign managers needed were always in the data -- they just weren't ever where managers were looking.

## Process

### 1. Discovery & Research

Reviewed usage analytics and interviewed campaign managers to identify which metrics they checked most frequently and how they used them.

### 2. Define

Framed the redesign around surfacing the highest-frequency metrics by default, rather than requiring users to configure their own view first.

### 3. Ideate & Wireframe

Explored several dashboard layouts that prioritized default visibility of key metrics over configurability.

### 4. Design & Prototype

Designed high-fidelity dashboard screens and a lightweight prototype to validate the new default layout with users.

### 5. Test & Iterate

Tested the prototype with campaign managers and adjusted which metrics appeared by default based on their feedback.

## Solution

The redesigned dashboard surfaces the metrics campaign managers check most often directly on load, removing the need to build a custom report just to see day-to-day performance. Custom reporting remains available for less frequent, deeper analysis.

## Learnings & Reflections

I initially assumed more configurability would be welcomed, but testing showed most users wanted fewer decisions, not more options. If I revisited this project, I'd validate the "smart defaults over configuration" direction earlier instead of building out a fuller customization system first.
