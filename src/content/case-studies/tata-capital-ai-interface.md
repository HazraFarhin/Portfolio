## Outcome & Impact

- Stakeholders approved the AI interface concept for the next release cycle
- Improved clarity around how and why the interface's recommendations were generated
- Established interaction patterns for surfacing AI confidence and reasoning across the product

## The Challenge

- Customers were hesitant to trust automated recommendations without understanding how they were generated
- Financial services users expected a higher bar of transparency than typical consumer AI products
- Existing patterns for AI-driven UI within the product were inconsistent and easy to misread

> Trust in an AI recommendation starts with understanding it, not just seeing it.

## Process

### 1. Discovery & Research

Reviewed customer feedback and held workshops with compliance and product stakeholders to understand trust and transparency requirements.

### 2. Define

Framed the core problem as one of explainability -- customers needed to understand the "why" behind a recommendation, not just the "what."

### 3. Ideate & Wireframe

Explored several ways to surface reasoning and confidence alongside AI-generated recommendations without overwhelming the interface.

### 4. Design & Prototype

Built an interactive prototype showing recommendations paired with lightweight, expandable explanations.

### 5. Test & Iterate

Tested the prototype with target users and refined how much explanation to show by default versus on demand.

## Solution

The final interface pairs each AI-generated recommendation with a concise, expandable explanation of the reasoning behind it, letting customers dig deeper only when they want to. The pattern was built to be reusable across other AI-assisted surfaces in the product.

## Learnings & Reflections

I underestimated how much the right amount of explanation mattered -- too much detail felt overwhelming, too little felt evasive. If I were to start over, I'd test explanation depth as its own variable earlier, rather than treating it as a secondary detail of the recommendation UI.
