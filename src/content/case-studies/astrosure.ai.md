## Tools Used

- Figma
- FigJam
- Maze

## Outcome & Impact

- Stakeholders approved the redesigned quoting flow for the next release cycle
- Clarified policy terminology at each step, reducing reliance on outside support channels
- Established a consistent visual system across quoting and claims screens

## The Challenge

- Policy and coverage details were presented in dense, hard-to-parse language throughout the quoting flow
- Customers frequently abandoned the claims flow partway through, often citing confusion about next steps
- The quoting and claims experiences used inconsistent visual patterns, making the product feel disjointed

> Customers weren't struggling with insurance -- they were struggling with how it was being explained to them.

## Process

### 1. Discovery & Research

Reviewed customer support tickets and interviewed policyholders to understand where quoting and claims language caused confusion.

### 2. Define

Framed the core problem as one of clarity -- customers needed to understand what they were buying and what to expect at each claims step.

### 3. Ideate & Wireframe

Sketched simplified layouts for presenting coverage details and claims status in plainer, more scannable language.

### 4. Design & Prototype

Built high-fidelity screens for the quoting and claims flows, introducing a shared visual system across both.

### 5. Test & Iterate

Tested the prototype with prospective and existing policyholders, refining terminology and layout based on comprehension issues observed in sessions.

## Solution

The redesigned platform presents coverage and claims information in plainer language with a consistent visual system spanning quoting through claims, so customers can follow their policy from purchase through resolution without switching mental models.

## Learnings & Reflections

I underestimated how much insurance-specific jargon had become invisible to the internal team -- terms that felt obvious to stakeholders were consistently confusing to test participants. Next time, I'd bring in outside participants for terminology review much earlier in the process.
